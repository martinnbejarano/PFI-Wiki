\chapter*{Abstract}

This work addresses the spread of disinformation on social media and the lack of a free tool, adapted to the Argentine context, that allows users to assess the veracity of a post at the moment they read it. To that end, Factum was developed: a Google Chrome extension for X that combines a natural language classifier fine-tuned on Spanish corpora, the public signals of the author's account, and the comparison of the claim against official sources, reference media outlets, and professional fact-checkers.

Each result comes with a justification and links to the sources that support it, so that users can verify it on their own. The solution was implemented as a functional prototype that integrates into the X timeline and is complemented by a web dashboard, which gathers each user's analysis history and offers organizations an aggregated view of trends. On 94 verified Argentine posts, the system's verdict reached a macro F1 score of 0.712, above the baseline (0.510) and below the 0.75 target, without flagging any true post as false. The classifier alone obtained 0.518, which shows the gap between the news in academic corpora and short social media posts and explains why the verdict relies on the comparison against sources. On-demand analysis takes 8.5~s at the median and 16.0~s at the 95th percentile, above the planned eight seconds. For claims that are repeated in other words, a semantic evidence memory reuses the sources already retrieved and, measured end to end in the local environment, resolves a hit in 40~ms at the 95th percentile; on the same 62 pairs used to choose its threshold, it reached a precision of 0.938 and a recall of 0.484. The usability test, with three participants, obtained a mean SUS score of 74.2.
